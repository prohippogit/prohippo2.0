PART ONE

The need of the hour

Indian income tax assessment moved onto a portal. The practice that serves it largely did not. Everything in this product follows from that gap.

ProHippo
The shift

Assessment is no longer a conversation. It is a clock.

Faceless assessment and faceless appeal removed the officer you could ring, the counter you could visit, and the adjournment you could ask for across a desk. What replaced them is a queue of dated documents on a website.

Service is deemed

A notice is served the moment it is uploaded to the e-filing portal and pushed to the registered email. Whether anyone opens it is not the department's problem.

Windows are short

Compliance notices routinely allow 7 to 15 days. An e-proceeding closes on the date printed on it. There is no counter open the next morning.

Processing is automatic

CPC processes returns u/s 143(1) and makes adjustments without asking. Each intimation starts its own rectification and appeal clocks.

The penalty is structural

A missed notice is not a rebuke. It is best-judgment assessment u/s 144, penalty proceedings, and a demand that becomes recoverable while you argue.

The asymmetry that defines the problem: the department's side of this is fully automated and never forgets. The practitioner's side is a person, a browser tab, and a memory of which client mentioned what.

ProHippo
The gap, in practice

Five ways a matter is lost today — none of them careless

1 · The notice reaches the client, not the professional

Service goes to the assessee's registered email and portal account. The client does not read the portal; they forward the email a week later, or after the hearing. The one person who could have acted was never told.

2 · Nobody logs into three hundred portals every morning

A working practice carries hundreds of PANs. Checking each e-filing account daily is the only reliable way to see a notice on the day it issues — and it is physically impossible by hand.

3 · One matter lives in four places

Portal PDFs in a downloads folder, instructions on WhatsApp, the reply in email, the hearing date in a diary. Nothing joins them, so context is rebuilt from scratch at every step — and lost entirely at every handover between staff.

4 · Limitation is counted by hand, from a date somebody has to find

Thirty days, sixty days, from service or from communication — computed off an order buried in a folder. A date computed one day late is an appeal that no longer exists.

5 · The work is done and never billed

Fees, receipts and outstanding follow-ups sit in a wholly separate system from the matters that generated them — so recovery depends on whoever remembers.

ProHippo
Why this year, specifically

From 1 April 2026, two limitation regimes run side by side

The Income-tax Act, 2025 does not merely renumber the sections. It changes the shape of the arithmetic every appeal deadline is computed with.

AppealAct, 1961Act, 2025
To CIT(A)30 days from service1 month from the end of the month of service
To ITAT60 days from communication2 months from the end of the month of communication

Which clock applies is decided by when the order was communicated — not by the assessment year. Limitation is procedural. An order communicated in June 2026 runs on the new Act even though the assessment it decides relates to A.Y. 2017-18. So for years to come, a single practice will hold live deadlines on both regimes at once.

What ProHippo does about it
  • Computes both regimes for every appealable order, always — never only the one it thinks is operative.
  • Marks which one governs and shows the date arithmetic on screen, so it is checkable rather than trusted.
  • Lets the practitioner override the communicated date to force either regime, for the cases where service is disputed.
  • Keeps the rules as data in one file, so a statutory change is an edit, not a rebuild.

The error that matters is one-directional

A deadline shown earlier than the true one costs a few days of comfort. A deadline shown later than the true one costs the appeal. The engine is built around that asymmetry — it shows both computations rather than quietly picking one.

Page 02 · Returns tab

Every filed return, and what CPC did to it

The year-by-year table

  • A.Y. and form, flagged where the filing was revised, defective, updated or condoned
  • Filed on, and whether it was e-verified and by what mode

When a document cannot be opened, it says why

"Add the date of birth to unlock", "the date on file doesn't unlock this PDF", "the portal only sends this one by e-mail for A.Y. 2016-17 and earlier". The practitioner is told whether it is theirs to fix.

The variance line

Under each intimation or rectification order, one sentence saying what it did to that year's position — a signed rupee figure, green or red, against the baseline it is measured from. A s.154 order is measured against the intimation it rectified; a s.143(1) against the return as filed. The baseline is part of the sentence, never a tooltip, because the same figure otherwise describes two different events.

Where the numbers come from

CPC's figure is read from the order's status, never by netting two fields against each other — because when a refund is set off u/s 245 the portal populates both, and netting them reports a demand on an order that says "there is no payment due". Where the portal sent no amount at all, the order's own printed total is used and labelled as such. An unrecognised status reports "could not be compared" rather than guessing.

The pre-filled worksheet

Form 35 or Form 36 under the 1961 Act — Form 99 or 115 under the 2025 Act — populated from the record already held: appellant, PAN, address, order particulars, section, assessment year, demand and the grounds skeleton.

Fully deterministic. No AI anywhere in it.

Limitation, fee and form selection are arithmetic on the record. There is no model in this path, no network call, and nothing that can differ between two runs. A statutory deadline is not a thing to be predicted.

It knows an order from its enclosures

The portal's "Download closure order" returns the real order bundled with the computation sheet and the notice of demand u/s 156 — all flagged as orders. ProHippo classifies each, because only the order itself attracts an appeal. A demand notice does not.

The route is decided, not guessed

Scrutiny and penalty orders route to CIT(A); a CIT(A) order routes to the ITAT. Orders whose onward forum is out of scope are not surfaced as false work.

Page 06 · Intimations & rectifications

What the practice owes its clients after CPC has been through them

It drills down, it does not sprawl

A searchable list of clients, one row each, saying how much is at stake → that client's orders, one row per assessment year → one order's full card, in a pop-up. On a real practice, one flat page of every order is hundreds of rows deep and tells nobody where to start.

Two more ways to cut it

By cause — every client hit by the same CPC adjustment, together, so one answer can be written once and used many times. By staff — what each person is holding, and how much of it they have finished.

Every order carries a decision

Accept it, rectify it, or appeal it — recorded against the order, so the page is a place to work rather than a place to look. Rectification and appeal windows are computed on the same engine the Appeals page uses.

Red and green mean one thing, everywhere

Positive is money coming back to the assessee. A red figure means CPC left them worse off than the baseline; green, better. One sign convention, set once at ingest, so the dashboard number and the Returns-tab number cannot disagree.

Read the order

One button reads the comparison table inside a s.143(1) or s.154 order, and the earlier years' demand in its annexures. It can also run automatically for red-flagged orders — off by default, switched on in Settings.

What is deliberately absent

  • No client messaging from this page. One sync can pull a dozen new PANs; a screen that could message off the back of that will one day message a dozen people nobody meant to contact.
  • No s.220(2) interest projection. It would be our arithmetic sitting beside figures the department stated, and here the two must never be confusable.

Page 08 · Communications

A document request is not a message. It is a checklist that sends messages.

Why that distinction is the whole design

If a request were just text in a log, then "ask for six documents, four arrive, chase the other two before the hearing" would have nowhere to live. Modelling it as an object with a lifecycle — drafted → sent → items arriving → complete — means reminders, multi-channel sending and per-item progress all fall out of one record.

The composer

  • Items pre-filled from what the notice called for, or from ready-made presets by proceeding type
  • Every item editable in place, reorderable, markable received or waived
  • A note per item, and a required/optional flag
  • Live preview of exactly what the client will read

In the client's own language

Twelve Indian languages — Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia, Urdu and English — shown in their own script beside the English name. Translation is of our sentences, with the client's details held back in placeholders. The letter itself is never sent anywhere to be translated.

The page

Four filters: Requests (drafts first, then sent), the full message log, WhatsApp and Email. The page header says how many drafts are unsent and how many requests are still awaiting documents.

The delivery log is append-only

One row per send attempt — channel, recipient, subject, template and a real delivery status carried back from the provider. One request can produce several rows: an email, then a WhatsApp hand-off, then a reminder, each pointing back at its parent.

Also here

A plain New message composer for the one-off note that is not a document request, and the same request composer reachable from any notice, any assessee, or any draft.

The morning's notices, in before anyone sits down

What it is

A desktop application for macOS and Windows, downloaded from inside ProHippo with OS-aware first-run instructions. It signs itself in from a device key held in the machine's keychain — issued from the app, revocable from the app — so no password is typed on a machine that runs unattended.

Parallel, and isolated

Several PAN syncs run at once, each inside its own isolated browser context with its own cookies and session — the desktop equivalent of the portal's own dual-login rule, but with every PAN a genuinely separate session, so portal sessions never collide.

Incremental by design

Before logging in, it reads what ProHippo already holds for that PAN, then fetches only what is new. A second sync is not a second download of the same four hundred files.

Unattended, on a timer that behaves

  • Starts with the operating system and syncs once at launch
  • Keeps going on a re-armed timer — every six hours by default — computed from the wall clock, so a laptop that slept through three of them syncs once on waking, not three times
  • The next run is drawn from a range around the interval, so the portal never sees the same schedule two days running
  • Quiet hours are respected
  • It will not run while another sync is in flight, and never runs at all unless it was switched on

Paced to look like a person

Staggered, randomised launches and per-request jitter — because a firm's residential connection making four hundred identical requests in eight seconds is a firm that gets blocked. Runs the machine's real Chrome where it can.

One number carries every practice

ProHippo is the sender; the practitioner's firm is named in the body. So a client who reports a message as spam does not damage that practice's deliverability — they damage everyone's. Consent, the opt-out list and the burst guards are not compliance theatre; they are what stops one careless practice taking the platform down for the rest.

Four guards on the new-notice alert

  • Only notices whose deadline has not already passed — a first sync finds four years of history, and nobody wants to be alerted to all of it
  • Only notices that came from the portal — not the one you just typed in
  • A claim on the DIN, so the same notice cannot alert twice
  • A burst cap, so a big sync cannot become a hundred messages

Integrations · Calendar and Tribunal mail

Into the diary the practitioner already keeps

Google Calendar

ProHippo creates its own calendar inside the practitioner's Google account and mirrors hearings and deadlines into it. Sync is one-way by design: ProHippo is the record of truth, and an event dragged in Google never silently moves a hearing here.

  • Connect and disconnect from Settings, with the last sync time on screen
  • Choose separately whether hearings and deadlines mirror
  • Any single hearing can be kept out of the sync
  • Uses the narrowest possible Google scope — the app can only touch the calendar it created, and can never read the practitioner's personal one
ITAT email ingest

Each practice gets one inbound address of its own. Anything sent to it that came from the Tribunal is read — appeal number, appellant, PAN, assessment year, hearing date and time, bench, filing date, case type — and offered on the Hearings page for one click of confirmation.

  • Register the address once with the Tribunal, or forward to it
  • Nothing reads anybody's mailbox — there is no mail account to connect, no OAuth scope to grant and no annual security review
  • A re-sent email cannot create a second matter
  • A superseded hearing is marked adjourned rather than duplicated
  • The sidebar badge counts what is waiting

Why "Call me" is the better door

The request arrives already authenticated, so ProHippo dials the number that account verified — never a number supplied in the request, which would turn the line into a free outbound dialler billed to us — and mints a signed token that lasts fifteen minutes, which is a call.

Every call is logged

Documents · Computation of Income

A computation sheet, generated from the return as filed

From the Returns tab, one click turns the ITR JSON already synced from the portal into a fully typeset Computation of Income PDF in the practice's brand — the document a client is given and a file is closed with.

Supported today

ITR-1A.Y. 2022-23 to 2026-27
ITR-2A.Y. 2022-23 to 2026-27
ITR-3A.Y. 2022-23 to 2026-27
ITR-5A.Y. 2025-26

A year without a mapper says so plainly, and names the years that do work — rather than offering a button that fails.

It must tie to the return, or it does not print

The generated document is validated against the filed return before it is rendered. If any head or total does not reconcile, generation fails loudly instead of handing over a computation that quietly disagrees with what was filed.

Deterministic, and specified

Same JSON in, same document out. No model, no network in the mapping — the only server call is the final render to PDF. The behaviour is written down in a specification the code is tested against; where the two disagree, the specification wins and the code is wrong.

Nothing is silently dropped

Fields the mappers do not yet handle are tracked rather than ignored, so an unmapped figure is a known gap, not an invisible one.

The boundary

Where AI is used — and where it deliberately is not

A model is allowed to read a PDF. Nothing else.

  • Summarise an order — and say which document it is: the assessment order, its computation sheet, or the notice of demand
  • Extract the documents a notice calls for, to seed the client checklist
  • Read the comparison table inside a s.143(1) or s.154 order
  • Translate our own sentences into the client's language — with their details held back in placeholders. The letter itself is never sent to be translated.

Each of these extracts something a PDF is the sole source of. That is the entire test for admitting a model.

Everything else is arithmetic on stated facts

  • A notice's PAN, assessment year, section, DIN and dates come from the portal's own structured data — not from reading the letter
  • Appeal limitation, the applicable regime, fees and forms are deterministic
  • The s.143(1) and s.154 variance is arithmetic on figures the department stated
  • The Computation of Income is a specified mapping, validated against the filed return

Why draw the line here. A summary that is slightly wrong costs a second read. A deadline that is slightly wrong costs the appeal. The two must never be produced by the same kind of process — so in ProHippo they are not.

An earlier build let a model pre-fill the notice intake form from a PDF. It was removed once notices began arriving through the sync with their fields already authoritative: re-reading them cost money to produce a worse answer.

Trust

The security posture, stated plainly

Data is per-account, at the database level

Every record lives under the signed-in user. Firestore rules — not application code — deny any read across accounts. A bug in a screen cannot leak another practice's file.

Portal passwords

Encrypted at rest with a key held in Firebase secrets, never exposed to the browser, and deletable at any time. The key is not derivable from the stored data.

Sign-in

Mobile OTP, email OTP or Google. A mobile number becomes usable for WhatsApp and the voice line only after it is proved by OTP — never one typed into a form.

The console cannot see client data. Ever.

ProHippo's own admin console reads account-level facts — firm, plan, status, usage counts. It has no access to any practitioner's assessees, notices, hearings or portal credentials. Not "restricted" — the rules grant nothing there, and no server function reads it.

The console shows "12 assessees". It never shows which twelve.

Admin is a signed claim, not a flag

It is signed into the token, so the rules and every server function agree without an extra read — and nothing the browser can write can grant it. There is deliberately no self-service way to become an admin.

Every privileged action is audited

Account changes, role grants and referral reversals are written to an append-only audit log with who did what and when.

Device keys

The desktop Connector signs in with a key that is issued, stored in the OS keychain, and revocable from the app — so a lost office machine is one click, not a password reset across every client.

Your data leaves when you ask

A full JSON backup on demand, CSV exports across the practice, and a documented data-deletion route.

The case, consolidated

Six reasons this is the need of the hour

1 · Compliance became a machine. The response did not.

The Department issues, serves, processes and penalises automatically. A practice answering it with a browser tab and a diary is not slow — it is structurally outmatched.

2 · Service is deemed, so attention must be manufactured

Nobody is coming to tell you a notice arrived. Something has to go and look, every day, across every PAN — and then put it in front of a human. That is the product.

3 · Two limitation regimes now run at once

From 1 April 2026 the appeal clock changes shape, and which clock governs depends on when the order was communicated. Counting that by hand, across a full practice, for years — is exactly the kind of arithmetic that should never be done by hand.

4 · The cost of a miss is asymmetric and unrecoverable

A lapsed appeal is not an inconvenience; it is a client's assessment becoming final. Software that prevents one such lapse a year has already paid for itself many times over.

5 · Clients live on WhatsApp, in their own language

A reminder in an inbox nobody opens is not a reminder. ProHippo delivers where attention already is, in twelve Indian languages, with consent recorded and delivery tracked.

6 · The tooling finally exists to do this honestly

Portal data is structured enough to be fetched rather than guessed. Documents can be read where a PDF is the only source. And the parts that must never be wrong — limitation, fees, computations — can stay fully deterministic and testable. That combination was not available a few years ago. It is now.
